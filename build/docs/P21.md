# P21 — V2.0, the estimator that measures instead of asserting

## Why this round

CORE §7 has carried one line of road that nothing had discharged:

> **V2.0** the live estimator: sample the actual input, run the real candidates on the real
> CPU, report measured time and ratio instead of folklore.

Every other item on the road was shipped. §5 named the thing it replaces in the same
breath — the eight method sentences ship *"one honest sentence each, static in v1.x — the
live estimator that measures your data on your CPU is V2.0"* — so the clause was a
standing admission that the popup, at the moment a person is choosing, **asserts**.

*"Very fast, and the archive stays large"* is true on average and unknowable about **your**
files. A tarball of already-compressed photographs and a tarball of source text disagree by
a factor of two about what zstd will do, and neither of them is the average. This round
replaces the average with a number taken on the machine in front of you.

It is a **version, not a revision**: `Cargo.toml` to `2.0.0`, `pkgrel` staying `1`, which
makes `release.yml`'s derivation accept exactly one tag — **`v2.0`**.

## What the measurements say

Measured **before** designing, because the design turns on them. AMD Ryzen 5 3450U, 8
threads, against this repository's own source tarball.

**Cost is one method, twice over.** At INDIUM's default levels on 2 MiB: lz4 ~15 ms, zstd
~35, gzip ~120, bzip2 ~202, xz 1098. LZMA2 tracks xz. Serially — which CORE §3 requires —
the eight together are about **2.7 s**, and roughly 2.2 s of that is xz and LZMA2 alone.
Nothing about the budget is a round number chosen for looks: it is what those two cost.

**A head sample is confidently wrong.** Sampling the first 1 MiB of a 4 MiB tarball
predicted zstd at **28.5%** where the true whole-archive ratio was **54.0%**. The head of
this repository's tarball is source text and its tail is already-compressed images, so the
first megabyte describes a different archive from the one being built. Head sampling does
not measure the input; it measures the input's first chapter, and reports it with a
straight face.

**Stratifying fixes most of it, and cannot fix xz.** Sixteen 64 KiB chunks spread evenly
across the same stream:

| Method | head 1 MiB | stratified 1 MiB | true, whole archive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| gzip | 29.7% | **54.8%** (−1.1) | 55.8% |
| zstd | 28.5% | **47.2%** (−6.8) | 54.0% |
| xz | 22.9% | **39.9%** (+13.7) | 26.2% |

gzip lands within a point. zstd within seven. **xz is out by 13.7 points and structurally
so** — LZMA's dictionary earns its ratio on long-range matches, and chopping a stream into
chunks is precisely the operation that destroys them. No arithmetic recovers them.

The conclusion the design is built on: **throughput samples honestly, ratio does not**, and
it fails worst for the method people choose *specifically* to get the smallest file. That
asymmetry is why sampled figures are marked and whole-input figures are not.

## What the finished estimator says

The numbers above were taken with shell pipes before any code existed. These were taken
**by the program**, through `the_eight_candidates_run_in_sequence_over_a_real_input`, over
this repository's own `src/` — 812.9 KiB, under the budget, so every figure is exact:

```
  Store    level  0 ·      8 ms ·  103.5%
  lz4      level  1 ·      5 ms ·   45.5%
  gzip     level  6 ·     45 ms ·   28.6%
  zstd     level  3 ·     16 ms ·   28.2%
  bzip2    level  9 ·    114 ms ·   20.3%
  xz       level  6 ·    571 ms ·   22.6%
  LZMA2    level  6 ·    566 ms ·   29.0%
  Deflate  level  6 ·     40 ms ·   31.5%
```

Three things in that table are worth the round on their own, and none of them is what
folklore would have said.

**Store is 103.5%.** A tar of small files is *larger* than the files, because every member
carries a 512-byte header and is padded to a 512-byte boundary. The ratio column can
legitimately exceed 100% and the program prints it rather than clamping it — a person
choosing Store for a directory of small files deserves to see that it costs them.

**bzip2 beats xz here**, 20.3% against 22.6%, on source text at their default levels. §5's
sentences say xz makes the smallest archive INDIUM can make, and on the average input that
is true; on *this* input it is not. That is the entire argument for the round in one row.

**LZMA2 is 29.0% where xz is 22.6%, at the same level, using the same algorithm.** The
difference is not the coder — it is that `tar.xz` compresses one concatenated stream while
this 7z writes members separately, so cross-file redundancy that xz exploits is invisible to
it. No sentence in §5 could have told anyone that, and no amount of reasoning about "LZMA2"
and "xz" being the same family would have predicted it. It took running them.

**The cost, measured rather than estimated:** 1365 ms in total for 813 KiB, of which xz and
LZMA2 are 1137 — **83%**. Scaled to the 2 MiB budget that is about **three and a half
seconds**, which is more than the 2.7 s the design was argued with. The pre-design figure
was taken on a different mix of content and is left above as what was known at the time;
this one is what the program does, and it is the number CORE §7 and the code now carry.

## The decisions, as ruled

Put to the maker with those numbers in hand.

| | Question | Ruling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | When it measures | **On demand**, a Measure action. The popup opens as it always did. |
| D2 | Sample budget | **Whole input at or under 2 MiB; stratified sample above it**, said in words. |
| D3 | The xz problem | **Show the figure, marked as an estimate.** Measured and estimated are told apart. |
| D4 | The eight sentences | **Numbers beside the sentence, both live.** CORE §5's table untouched. |
| D5 | Where the bytes come from | **Staged files, then the open archive, else the button is dead and says why.** |
| D6 | Budget, given serial execution | **2 MiB stands** at ~2.7 s, rows filling as they land. |

D4 was the cheapest and it was chosen on merit rather than on cost: a number says how much,
a sentence says what for, and *"Very slow, and the archive is as small as INDIUM can make
it"* is still the reason to pick xz after its ratio has been printed. Leaving §5's table
alone also left `every_method_verdict_is_core_section_five_verbatim` green and untouched,
which is the shape of a decision that was already right.

## What was built

**`src/estimate.rs`**, and therefore a row in CORE §3's architecture table — `src/lib.rs`'s
`the_architecture_table_names_every_module_and_nothing_else` cross-checks `pub mod` against
`src/` against that table in both directions, so the module could not compile a green suite
until the document named it. The document moved first, as it is supposed to.

**It owns no format knowledge.** Every candidate goes through the same `Sink` Apply goes
through — `arch::Writer` for tar and zip, `sevenz::Writer` for 7z — into a scratch file that
is measured and deleted. A method added to `METHODS` is measured without this module being
touched. **No new crate, no new FFI, no new `unsafe`:** `std::time::Instant` is the entire
toolkit, and CORE §2's one-sentence rule was never reached for.

**Where the scratch file lives.** `platform::scratch` gained a third `Kind` — `Estimate`,
prefix `es`. Inventing a temp path would have been two lines; routing through `Scratch`
brings the RAM-or-disk rule, the process id in the name that P8 put there, the launch sweep
and the drop guard, all of which the estimator needs for the same reasons copy-out does. At
2 MiB it always routes to `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR`, which is tmpfs, so the write is memory.

**Exact under the budget, marked above it.** At or under 2 MiB the real members go through
the real writer in plan order, and the resulting figure is *not an estimate*: it is the size
Apply would produce, container overhead included. A test proves that claim rather than
asserting it — `an_exact_measurement_is_the_size_the_writer_actually_produces` builds the
same members twice, once through `measure` and once straight through the writer, and
requires the two to agree **to the byte**. Above the budget one synthetic member holds
chunks lifted at an even stride, which keeps container overhead constant across the eight so
the *comparison* stays fair even where the absolute ratio drifts, and every figure from it
carries `~`.

**The read is capped, not just the sample.** Stratifying staged files is free — a file
seeks. An archive does not: libarchive decompresses sequentially and even `skip_data` on a
solid stream does the work, so spreading a sample across a 2 GiB `.tar.xz` would mean
decompressing 2 GiB before the first candidate ran. The archive source therefore strides
across the *entry list* and stops at a walked-bytes cap. Without it the feature would have
been unusable on exactly the archives worth re-compressing.

**Never encrypted.** `sevenz::Writer` refuses `encrypt: true` with no passphrase, and CORE
§9 keeps passwords typed per use and never held, so there is none at Measure time. Asking
for a password in order to time a compressor would have been an outrageous trade for a
figure AES-256 barely moves. The measuring recipe carries `encrypt: false` and a test
(`a_candidate_is_never_encrypted`) drives the one method that *could* encrypt to prove it.

**One worker, in sequence.** CORE §3: *"the UI thread and one worker."* There is no pool,
which is what makes the budget 2.7 s rather than one second, and what makes Measure a button
rather than something the popup does on opening — three seconds of CPU spent answering a
question nobody asked would have been a worse program than one that asserts. Each figure is
sent the moment it lands and the rows fill one at a time.

**Its own cancellation flag**, never `self.cancel`, which belongs to a rebuild. A
`Cancellable` reader wrapper stops mid-member, because cancellation checked only between
members leaves one 2 MiB xz candidate uninterruptible for over a second — long enough that
closing the popup would visibly fail to close it. Both of the popup's close paths cancel,
and **Apply and extraction preempt a measurement**: New Archive is an `egui::Window` rather
than a `Modal`, so the tray strip stays clickable underneath it and real work genuinely can
start while the candidates run. The estimate is advisory; the rebuild is not.

**The window.** The figures sit in a monospace lane against the right edge of each method
row — last in the row, because a right-to-left layout claims whatever width is left and
claiming it before the `AES-256` tag would leave that tag nowhere to go. Right-aligned so
the columns hold as digits change (§4). Told in text and never in colour: §6 refuses a sixth
colour by name and *"in particular no green for success"*, so a good ratio reads exactly
like a bad one and the number does the talking. **The level is printed with the figure**,
because the slider goes on moving afterwards and a figure that does not say which level it
was taken at becomes quietly false the moment it is left behind.

**Measure sits on the METHOD heading's own line**, which is why `theme::list_height`'s
chrome constant did not have to move: a row of its own would have cost the method list a row
of height at every display scale, and the heading line was already there with nothing on its
right.

**New Archive had to become re-openable, and that was the round's real structural work.**
`stage_creation` cleared the queue, so pressing `N` a second time discarded every file
staged for adding — invisible until now, because the popup could not usefully be re-opened
over a staged creation anyway. But D5 says the estimator measures staged files, and files
arrive *after* the popup closes. So: `Queue::set_creation` replaces the staged `Task::Create`
**in place**, preserving its documented invariant (*"Only ever first, and only ever one"*),
and `open_new_archive` pre-fills from the staged recipe instead of resetting to Balanced.

## What building it found, that reasoning about it did not

### 1. `Path::file_stem` would have built `photos.tar.tar.gz`

Re-opening the popup has to put the *stem* back in the Name field, and every compressed tar
carries two dots. `file_stem` removes one: `photos.tar.gz` becomes `photos.tar`, the popup
re-stages that plus `.tar.gz`, and offers to build a name nobody typed from a field nobody
edited. Only `extension_for`'s table knows that `.tar.gz` is *one* extension, so the split
goes through it — extracted to `stem_of` precisely so it could be pinned, and it is, across
all eight methods and a stem that legitimately contains dots.

### 2. Two tests deleted each other's scratch file

`measure` writes and deletes a file called `candidate`, and the first two tests to use it
both put it in `temp_dir()`. Cargo runs tests in parallel; one removed the other's file
mid-measurement, and the failure read as *"the candidate went missing"* — which is exactly
the collision P8 fixed in `platform::scratch` by putting the process id in the directory
name. Arrived at here the same way P8 arrived at it: by watching it happen. The tests now
take a pid-named directory each.

### 3. A right-to-left layout claims the row

The figure lane was written before the `AES-256` tag and swallowed the width it needed. It
is a one-line ordering fact with no error message attached — the tag simply stops being
drawn — and it is written down in the code beside the fix so the next thing added to that
row does not rediscover it.

(The lane itself is gone — see *The tenth popup* below. The fact is kept because it is about
`egui::Layout::right_to_left`, not about the lane, and the next thing added to a row in this
program will meet it again.)

### 4. The estimator's honesty is testable, and the head-sampler proves it

`the_sample_is_drawn_from_across_the_input_and_not_from_its_head` fills each member with its
own index, so the sample's *contents* say which members it came from. Sabotaged into a head
sampler, it fails with `saw members [0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7]` out of forty. That failure was
produced deliberately before the test was trusted, as was the typography test's.

### 5. The button's enabled state was cloning the whole archive, sixty times a second

`estimate_source` resolved the input *and* answered "can Measure run?", and the popup asked
it the second question every frame — which meant cloning every staged path and, on the
archive branch, **the entire entry list**, on every frame the popup was open. On a
hundred-thousand-entry archive that is hundreds of megabytes a second of allocation spent
deciding whether a button should be grey. It is split now: `estimate_refusal` reads counts
and flags every frame, `estimate_source` builds the input once, on the click. Nothing in
the test suite would have caught this; it is the kind of defect only reading the frame path
finds.

### 6. The dead button's sentence was written where nobody could ever read it

D5 says the button is dead **and says why**. It said why by putting the sentence in the
status bar when clicked — and a disabled button never reports a click, so the one person who
needed the sentence was the one person who could not summon it. The refusal is drawn beside
the button now, always, which is what "says why" had to mean all along.

### 7. The walk cap was charging itself only for what it kept

`WALK_CAP` exists because `skip_data` on a solid stream is not free — libarchive runs the
filter sequentially, so skipping a member decompresses it just as reading it would. The walk
then added `entry.size` to its running total **only for the members it selected**, which
makes the cap unreachable on precisely the archive it was written for: thirty-two sampled
members of a 2 GiB `.tar.xz` weigh a couple of megabytes between them, so the walk would
sail past a 64 MiB cap while decompressing all 2 GiB to reach the last of them. The
accounting now charges every member the walk passes, before the selection decides anything.

The reason it survived the first pass is worth more than the fix: at 64 MiB the only input
that exercises the cap is one too large to build in a test, so a cap that never fired would
have tested green forever. The walk therefore takes its cap as a parameter — `from_archive`
passes `WALK_CAP`, and `the_walk_pays_for_the_members_it_skips_and_stops_when_it_has_spent_the_cap`
passes 256 KiB over four hundred small members, where twelve are skipped for every one read.
Charged properly the walk stops around member 25; charged only for reads it reaches **387 of
400**, which is what it printed when the old accounting was put back to check.

### 8. Figures outlived the input they described

The plan's own words: *"No cache of results between openings — a measurement that outlives
the data it measured is folklore again."* `cancel_estimate` stopped the worker and dropped
the receiver but left `estimates` populated, so closing the popup, staging different files
and re-opening showed the old archive's numbers under the new input's sentence — folklore
with a stopwatch on it, which is the exact failure this round exists to remove. It clears
the figures now, on every path that calls it: the two popup closes, Apply, and extraction.

This one is **held by construction rather than by a test**, and the record should say so:
`Indium::new` takes an `eframe::CreationContext`, so there is no way to stand one up in a
unit test without a live GL context, and nothing in the tree does. The project's UI-state
tests sit one level down, on the pure functions — `set_creation`, `stem_of`, `figure_of` —
which is why P21's re-opening behaviour is pinned at `Queue::set_creation` and this is not.
Naming the gap is the honest option; inventing a harness for it is a different round.

### 9. At the window it was seventeen points wrong, and the tests all passed

The plan's third verification step was the one that mattered: *"the estimator's own figure
for zstd on a known input, checked against the archive Apply actually builds from it. If they
disagree, the estimator is wrong and the round is not done."* Run against this repository's
own 6.3 MiB tarball, the popup said **zstd 37.7%** where the truth is **54.6%**, and **bzip2
31.5%** where the truth is **49.4%**. Seventeen points optimistic — which is worse than the
folklore sentence it replaced, because it arrives dressed as a measurement.

Two defects, compounding, and neither visible to a test that was written:

**The sample was weighted by member count, not by bytes.** The walk strided the *entry list*
— thirty-two members spread evenly across eighty-eight. That is a spread, and the spread test
proved it was a spread, but an archive of source is eighty text members beside eight binary
ones, so an even sample of the list is nearly all text. The sample said what a folder of
source compresses to, and the archive is mostly PNGs.

**And then the sample was sampled again.** `from_archive` handed back members plus the
archive's total, and the caller passed both to `narrow` — which strided 580 KiB of held bytes
with a 190 KiB stride computed from the 6.3 MiB those bytes came from, keeping **74 KiB of
the 2 MiB budget** and throwing away seven eighths of what the walk had just paid to
decompress. That is why the window said *"estimated from 72.7 KiB"* and reported 0 ms.

The fix is one idea, not two: **stride 64 KiB chunks at even *byte* positions, during the
walk**, exactly as `narrow` does for staged files, and hand back a finished `Input` so no
second reduction can run. Chunks land in proportion to the bytes each member owns, so
composition is reproduced by construction and no member has to be chosen at all. A third
defect surfaced writing it: a chunk that ran off the end of a member was truncated there,
which hands back an 8 KiB scrap among small members and a full 64 KiB among large ones — the
same count-weighting through a different door. A chunk is 64 KiB of the *concatenation* now,
carried across the boundary, in both this walk and `narrow`.

Measured after, against the same truth:

| | gzip:6 | bzip2:9 | xz:6 | zstd:3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| truth | 56.4 | 49.9 | 26.5 | 54.7 |
| before | — | 31.5 (**−17.9**) | 33.5 (+7.0) | 37.7 (−16.9) |
| after | 57.1 (**+0.7**) | 52.0 (**+2.1**) | 33.5 (+7.0) | 39.9 (**−14.8**) |

and the budget is spent: **2.0 MiB**, not 72.7 KiB.

**zstd is still 14.8 points out, and that one does not have a fix.** It is not noise and it is
not a chunk size chosen badly — it is the methods disagreeing about the single property a
sample cannot preserve, which is how long the stream is. LZMA's 8 MiB dictionary earns its
ratio on long-range matches, so chopping the stream destroys them and xz reads *worse* than
it will be. zstd at level 3 has a 2 MiB window: it cannot span a 6.3 MiB input but it spans
the whole of a 2 MiB sample, so it reads *better* than it will be. `zstd -3 --long=27` on the
whole tarball returns 35.9% against 54.0% plain, which is the same 18 points seen from the
other side and is the proof.

Sweeping the geometry settles that no setting wins, because the two errors move in opposite
directions:

| chunks | zstd | gzip | xz | bzip2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 64 × 32 KiB | +1.8 | +1.1 | **+19.5** | +2.8 |
| **32 × 64 KiB** (shipped) | −6.9 | −0.4 | +13.1 | +0.2 |
| 16 × 128 KiB | −14.5 | −0.7 | +6.0 | −0.8 |
| 8 × 256 KiB | **−20.3** | −4.1 | +1.2 | −4.7 |

(That sweep is over the tar's bytes; the shipped sampler walks the members' contents, which
is why its zstd row reads −14.8 rather than −6.9. Shifting where the chunks land moves zstd
by eight points on this input all by itself — which is its own argument for the mark.)

**32 × 64 KiB is kept**, and not by default: it is the geometry whose errors are the ones the
maker was shown when he ruled D3 — gzip 1.1, zstd 6.8, xz 13.7 — and the sweep reproduces
those numbers almost exactly. Trading zstd's error for xz's is not an improvement, it is a
different archive's preference.

**What stands open, and it is his:** D3 ruled the figure is shown marked, on evidence that
sampled ratio could be ~14 points out. It can, and on this archive it is — but the mark says
*"this is an estimate"*, not *"this may order the methods wrongly"*, and here it does: the
sample ranks zstd ahead of bzip2 (39.9 against 52.0) where the truth ranks bzip2 ahead of
zstd (49.9 against 54.7). A reader choosing the smallest file from a sampled column can be
sent to the wrong method. Named here rather than resolved, because the ruling is his.

## The tenth popup, after the figures were seen

Everything above was written before the estimator had been looked at. It was correct, it was
tested, and the first thing the maker said about it was that he could not read it.

**He was right, and the reason is worth writing down.** The figures went into a lane on each
method row at **11 px in `TEXT_MUTED`** — the smallest and dimmest text anywhere in the popup
— beside a verdict sentence that had already claimed the width, under a method name set at
14 px bold. The round's entire payload landed in the least readable element on screen. The
lane was chosen because it was the space that existed, which is a reason to put something
*somewhere* and never a reason to put it *there*. No test can catch this; nothing was broken.
It was simply the wrong place, and it took one look to know.

**The answer was his, and it is better than a bigger font**: leave New Archive as it was —
verdicts and nothing else — and let Measure open a popup of its own that holds only the
measurements. `main window → New Archive → Measure`. In the same breath he lifted the
constraint that had ruled it out: *"i havent capped popup counts be free"*. CORE §4 counts
**ten** where it counted nine.

Three questions were put with previews, and all three answered:

| | Question | Ruling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E1 | When it runs | **On open**, and the figures are **kept while New Archive lives**. *Measure again* re-runs them; closing New Archive discards them. |
| E2 | Is it actionable | **Clicking a row picks that method** and closes the popup — you measured in order to choose. |
| E3 | Columns | **Level, time, size, ratio.** There is finally room for the produced size, which is the number a ratio is a percentage *of*. |

### What it took, and what it found

**The stack is a second slot, not a `Vec`.** `Indium::over: Option<Popup>` beside `popup`,
holding only `Measure` and only over `NewArchive`. `measure::show` guards on **both**, and
that is not belt-and-braces: sixteen sites assign `app.popup` by hand without going through
`close_popup`, New Archive is an `egui::Window` and not a modal so the sidebar under it stays
clickable, and `Ctrl+O` fires over anything.

**Guarding the draw was not enough, and this is the finding.** A guard stops the popup being
*painted* over a popup it was never about; it does not stop the state from persisting. Click
Settings from under an open Measure and `over` stays set — press `N` a minute later and the
Measure popup comes back, over figures measured from an input that has since changed. The
sweep therefore lives in `ui()`, on the line beside the one that re-arms `focus_given_to` for
exactly the same reason and by exactly the same argument. It clears the figures with it,
which is what makes E1's *"discarded when New Archive closes"* true on **every** close path
rather than on the four that call `close_popup`. That half was already leaking before this
round; it was invisible only because the figures were drawn inside the popup that was going
away.

**`over` deliberately does not take part in `focus_given_to`.** The re-arm compares that flag
against `popup` alone, so a `wants_initial_focus` keyed on an over-popup would be reset every
frame and hold focus forever. The Measure popup has no text field, so it simply does not ask;
teaching the re-arm about `over` is the price of the first over-popup that does.

**The row click is four assignments, not two.** Choosing a method sets the method, resets the
level, drops encryption when the method cannot carry it, and recomputes which preset chip is
lit. A second copy in `measure.rs` would have forgotten the last two in the usual way, so
there is one `newarchive::choose_method` and both popups call it.

**Two column widths were wrong, and arithmetic found them, not the eye.**
`util::format_bytes` divides until the value is under 1024 and prints one decimal — so
`1073741823`, one byte short of a gibibyte, divides to 1023.99998, **rounds up** to `1024.0`
and comes back as `1024.0 MiB`: ten characters where nine was budgeted. The size sweep in
`every_measured_column_holds_its_width` is exhaustive over every power of two *and its
predecessor* for that reason; three hand-picked sizes would have missed it and the ratio
column would have shifted on somebody's screen.

**And a ratio can genuinely exceed a thousand per cent.** `input_bytes` counts the members'
own bytes, so one staged byte against `Store`'s 10 KiB tar frame is 1 024 000% — true, and
about a header's fixed cost rather than about compression. Above 999.9% the cell reads
`>999%`, which is the honest report and the one that fits.

**All eight rows stand from the first frame**, their cells filling as candidates land. Growing
the table would have reflowed it eight times and moved every row under a pointer that was
reading it — motion in all but name, which CORE §6's fourth dated refusal is against. It also
makes *"one row at a time as they land"* mean something a fixed-height table can keep.

**`set_width` is a trap in a Modal.** It fixes both bounds, and a table wider than its bound
does not overflow in egui — it *wraps*, which turns a grid into rubble. The popup sets a
minimum and every row asks not to be wrapped.

**`theme::foot` cannot be used here**, and the reason is one number: it pulls back by `PAD`
(12) to escape `window_margin`, and an `egui::Modal` is inset by `menu_margin` (8). The band
would have overshot by four points on three edges. The foot is a plain row, as the password
prompt's is.

### The count that nothing was holding

The audit found **no test anywhere enforced nine popups** — and the count had already drifted
twice: §4 said seven while the code had eight, and a doc comment in `ui/mod.rs` said seven and
counted eight in the same sentence. P12 straightened both by hand.

`the_popup_list_and_core_agree_about_how_many_there_are` now reads all three places the number
is written: §4's opening sentence *in words*, §4's numbered list, and `Popup::ALL`. It also
checks the list is numbered 1..=n in order, and that no variant is pasted twice into `ALL` —
which is the one way a length check could be satisfied while wrong. `std::mem::variant_count`
is nightly-only, which is why `ALL` exists at all.

### The button was live over an archive that did not exist

Found by surveying the creation flow after the maker reported friction in it, and fixed here
rather than deferred, because it is a defect in the popup this round shipped.

`stage_creation` adopts the recipe's path the moment Create is pressed — the window takes the
title, the tray appears, and `archive_path` becomes `Some` for a file that has never been
written. `estimate_refusal` asked only `archive_path.is_none()`, so over a freshly staged
creation with nothing added yet **Measure was enabled**. Pressing it sent the worker to
`Reader::open` on a path with no file behind it, libarchive's raw error went to the status
bar, and the Measure popup sat there with eight blank rows reading *"Nothing has been measured
yet."* — the failure and the surface that provoked it in two different zones, in two different
voices.

The refusal now has a fourth case, and it is the honest one: *"This archive has not been read:
there is nothing to measure yet."* It is ordered **before** the encryption case deliberately —
nothing has told us there are encrypted members, so the password sentence would be a guess at
the reason given confidently.

**The decision moved out of `Indium` to make it testable**, and that is the more useful half.
It is now `estimate_refusal_for(has_adds, has_path, unread, locked)`, a free function of four
booleans, and `measure_refuses_wherever_it_has_nothing_to_weigh_and_says_which` covers every
state including the three that must *not* refuse. The gate had been wrong for a whole round
with a green suite, because it lived on a type no unit test can construct. Four booleans and a
sentence each is not something that has to be unreachable.

#### The same defect, one door over

The first version of this fix was wrong, and the way it was wrong is worth more than the fix.

It passed `creation().is_some() && archive_info.is_none()` — a faithful description of the
phantom *as first met*, through Create. But `discard_tasks` clears the queue and does not
touch `archive_path`: after `N` → Create → **Discard**, `creation()` is `None`, the path is
still adopted, no file exists, and the button was live again. The identical defect, reachable
through a second door, in the commit whose message says it was fixed.

The suite stayed green throughout, and could not have gone otherwise. Moving the *branch* off
`Indium` did not move the *mapping* off `Indium`, and the mapping is where the mistake was.
Every row of the test asserts on booleans handed to it; nothing asserts that the program
computes those booleans from the right fields. The test now says so in its own docstring,
because the next person to trust it should know which half it holds.

The mapping is now one fact rather than two — `archive_info.is_none()`, "nothing at this path
has ever been read" — which covers the phantom, the phantom after Discard, a listing still in
flight and a listing that failed. `entries` is empty in all four and the walk is built from
`entries`, so there is no state among them where measuring could have answered. Naming the
condition after what is *known* rather than after the story that produced it is what made the
second door close without being enumerated.

### What is held by construction and not by a test

`Indium::new` wants an `eframe::CreationContext`, so no unit test can build the application.
The second slot, the Esc precedence, the sweep and the row click are therefore verified at the
window and nowhere else — the same honest limit this document already records for
`cancel_estimate`. What *is* testable is the grid, and it is: every method, all three states
of a row, both marks, every power of two the size column can be handed, and the level and time
columns across the estimator's own stated domain.

## The typography line, tried and judged

Raised separately from the estimator and carried here because it is one line and one look.

**The window's text was never un-antialiased, and nothing was missing.** Read rather than
assumed, in `epaint-0.36.1`: `skrifa` pulls the CFF outline, hinting is on by default
(`Target::Smooth`), `vello_cpu` fills the path into a coverage pixmap — *that* is the
antialiasing — and the coverage runs through `TwoCoverageMinusCoverageSq`, which epaint
names `DARK_MODE_DEFAULT` and describes as *"very similar to a gamma of 0.5, but produces
sharper text."* INDIUM gets that one by construction, because `install_visuals` starts from
`Visuals::dark()`. `round_text_to_pixels` is on. There was no AA to add.

**Worth recording so nobody re-investigates: `feathering` is not the text setting.** It is
the one that sounds like it, and `tessellator.rs` says flatly *"This setting does not affect
text."* It smooths shape edges; raising it would blur the rules and leave every glyph
exactly as it was.

**What was plausibly wrong for this window specifically** is `subpixel_binning`, default
`true`: four fractional horizontal offsets per glyph, bought for even kerning, and epaint is
candid about the price — *"It also lead to text looking more blurry."* CORE §6 puts the
whole window in monospace on a fixed advance, so INDIUM **cannot collect that benefit and
was paying the blur for it at every size.** One line in `install_visuals`, set on `v` rather
than through `Options` because `text_options` lives on `Visuals` — which means the existing
`all_styles_mut` write covers both styles for exactly the reason the note beneath it gives.

`the_window_does_not_pay_for_kerning_it_cannot_collect` pins it, because the upstream
default is `true` and an egui upgrade would otherwise turn it back on silently. A second
test pins hinting and the dark-mode ramp as *decisions* rather than omissions, so a future
round tempted to "add antialiasing" is told it is already there.

**The verdict is the maker's, by eye, at 100% / 125% / 150%** — a test cannot tell anyone
that text got sharper. If the A/B refuses it, the line comes out, CORE §6 is not touched,
and the finding above still stands as the reason nobody need look again.

## Deviations

- **The plan named a `/dev/null` + `archive_filter_bytes` fallback** in case `Scratch`'s
  lifecycle did not fit. It fitted: `route` is pure and `begin`/`discard` are `&mut self` on
  the UI thread, so the UI thread makes the directory and the worker is handed a `PathBuf`.
  No new extern was declared and the fallback was not needed.
- **The plan expected the chrome constant to move.** Putting Measure on the heading line
  instead of a row of its own meant `theme::list_height(ctx, 452.0, 210.0)` did not have to
  change, which is a better outcome than measuring it again.
- **Extraction preempts a measurement as well as Apply.** The plan named only Apply. The
  hazard is identical and the fix is the same line, so leaving extraction out would have
  been a gap rather than a smaller scope.
- **The archive source strides bytes, where the plan said to stride the entry list.** The
  plan's words were *"the archive source stratifies **across the entry list**"*, and D2 was
  ruled on that description. It is overridden here on the authority of the plan's own third
  verification step — the estimator disagreed with the archive by seventeen points, and that
  step says a disagreement means the round is not done. Striding the list is what caused the
  disagreement; see the finding above. The ruling D2 actually made — whole input under the
  budget, stratified sample above it, said in words — is untouched. Recorded because the
  maker approved the letter as well as the spirit, and only the spirit survived.

## What this round does not do

- **No parallelism.** CORE §3 fixes threading at the UI thread and one worker. The 2.7 s is
  the cost of obeying it, and it is the maker's accepted trade.
- **No new dependency, no benchmark crate, no external binary** (CORE §2, §9).
- **No eleventh popup, and no keybinding for the tenth.** The round opened one popup, by the
  maker's lifting of the cap and after seeing why the alternative did not work. Measure is
  reached from New Archive's button alone, so §4's keyboard table and `keys::ROWS` are
  untouched and their test stays green.
- **No third level of nesting.** One popup may stand on one other; the guard on both slots
  and the sweep in `ui()` are what keep it that way.
- **No Settings group.** The budget is a constant, not a knob, and the settings panel's
  three groups are pinned by a test.
- **No cache of results between openings.** §9 refuses a database, and a measurement that
  outlives the data it measured is folklore again.
- **It does not touch the yazi plugin, and it closes none of P19's or P20's open boxes.**
