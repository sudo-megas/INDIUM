# P9 — The popup stops being the window wearing a different hat

*Answers the maker's complaint that a popup was still not visibly a different surface from the
window behind it, and repairs the older fault that complaint uncovered: a popup taller than its
window lost its own title and its own button. When this file and CORE.md disagree, CORE.md wins.*

**Goal in one sentence:** a popup wears three grounds of its own — a band that names it, a body
that holds it, a band that carries what it is about to do — in a hue no zone of the window uses,
and it never again grows past the edges of the window it floats in — shipped inside **`v1.0`**.

P8.md's closing line reads *"This document makes no claim about whether there is a P9. Two
documents have now ended with one and been wrong, and the honest form of that sentence is the one
that is not written."* That was the right sentence, and this is the milestone it left room for.
P9 exists because P7 §3 was measured and still wrong in use: it made popups lighter than every
zone and declared the job done, the maker looked at the result and said the popup *"still failed
to distinguish new window"*, and lightness alone turned out not to be the axis a person reads. A
milestone that answers "I still cannot tell these apart" is not a milestone about taste.

**Not in P9:** a second theme, or a theme setting — CORE §9 forbids both and P9 asks for neither.
No new popup, no popup removed, no change to what any popup does. Nothing about the window's own
five zones, which keep every colour P7 gave them. And no change to the type, the spacing, the
radii or the line weights: P9 spends hue and height and nothing else.

## 1. Lightness was the wrong axis

P7 §3 is titled *"Popups that are not the window"*, and by its own measure it succeeded: it gave
the popup `#4A1038`, a ground lighter than every zone, on the argument that *a popup covers every
zone, so it should sit above every zone*. The argument is sound. The ladder it produced is real —
six grounds, each about one and a half times the linear luminance of the one below.

It did not work, and the maker said so in one sentence: the popup *"still failed to distinguish
new window"*. The reason is not that the step was too small. It is that a person looking at a
screen full of aubergine does not read a sixth shade of aubergine as *a different kind of thing*.
They read it as the same thing, slightly lit. Lightness is the axis the theme had already spent
five times over, and a sixth spend on the same axis buys almost nothing.

So P9 spends the axis that was untouched. The maker chose the colours himself and sent them
marked on a screenshot — `#0F52BA` across the title band, `#4682B4` for the body, marked in three
separate places, `#0047AB` for the foot — and then said the thing that decided the rest of this
section:

> *"i thought ubuntu aubergine is unique and exceptional but we have to provide contrast"*

That is the maker overruling his own palette preference on a legibility argument, and it is
recorded here in his words because it is the whole warrant for a blue surface in an aubergine
program.

### What the chosen blue cost, and what was spent instead

`#4682B4` cannot be used as given. Measured against the text that already exists on a popup:

| Text on `#4682B4` | Contrast |
| --- | --- |
| `TEXT_MUTED` `#999997` | **1.44:1** |
| `TEXT` `#EEEEEC` | 3.54:1 |
| pure white, the ceiling | **4.11:1** |

The last row is the one that settles it. On a ground that light, *no* choice of text colour
reaches 4.5:1 — the brightest ink that exists tops out at 4.11. The muted text would have been
invisible and the primary text would have been marginal, and neither could have been fixed by
picking a different ink.

The fix keeps the maker's colours and spends only their lightness. **Scaling linear RGB by a
constant preserves chromaticity exactly** — the hue and the saturation are untouched; only the
luminance moves. So all three were scaled by the one constant that lands the body on the
luminance P7's aubergine popup already had: 0.02131 against `#4A1038`'s 0.02112, which is as
close as eight bits per channel allows and 0.9% away from identical:

| Role | Maker's pick | Shipped | `TEXT_MUTED` contrast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `POPUP_HEAD` | `#0F52BA` | `#02173F` | 6.15:1 |
| `POPUP` | `#4682B4` | `#132A3D` | 5.16:1 |
| `POPUP_FOOT` | `#0047AB` | `#00133A` | 6.37:1 |

Because the body sits at the old popup's luminance, **the six-ground ladder and every contrast
assertion P7 wrote stand unchanged**. Nothing else in the theme had to move. The surface became
unmistakable and the arithmetic underneath it did not notice.

## 2. The three grounds

A popup is not one surface. It is three, and each answers a different question:

- **`POPUP_HEAD`, the band across the top.** What this is. It is *darker* than the popup's own
  body, on the maker's instruction, and the reason survives inspection: the band is a lid, not a
  highlight. It also replaced something — P7 put Aubergine here to mean *this is the active
  item*, and that meaning stopped being available the moment the popup stopped being aubergine,
  because an aubergine band on a blue popup is the window's own colour sitting on top of the
  thing covering the window.
- **`POPUP`, the body.** What it holds.
- **`POPUP_FOOT`, the band across the bottom.** What is about to happen. It is the darkest of the
  three deliberately: the foot carries the sentence naming the consequence and the button that
  causes it, and Ubuntu Orange on an Apply or a Create has the most to push against there.

`install_visuals` also hands `widgets.open` the head's ground, so a combo box standing open reads
as the same kind of surface as the band above it rather than as plain body.

The foot is drawn by one helper rather than by six copies, and the interesting part of it is the
**negative outer margin**. `egui::Window` resolves to `Frame::popup`, whose inner margin insets
the whole of its content; a footer that respected that inset would be a floating strip with
popup-coloured gutters down both sides and along the bottom — which is a panel, not a foot.
Pulling back by exactly the inner margin puts the band's edges back on the popup's edges, and the
value pulled back is `PAD`, the same constant `install_spacing` gives that margin, so the two can
never drift apart.

**One popup is deliberately not three grounds.** The password prompt is an `egui::Modal`, not an
`egui::Window`; it takes the new body colour for free, because `Frame::popup` reads `window_fill`,
but it has no title band and no foot. That is right rather than an omission: it asks one question,
it holds one field, it cannot outgrow anything, and a lid and a foot on a two-line modal would be
furniture. It is written down here so the next person counting popups does not find six of seven
and assume a miss.

## 3. The popup that outgrew its window

The recolouring uncovered a fault older than itself, and worse than itself.

**`egui::Window` sizes itself from its content and is then clipped by the viewport rather than
shrunk to it.** A popup whose content grew past the window did not get a scroll bar; it got cut.
It lost its title band off the top and its foot off the bottom — which is to say it lost its name
and the button it exists for, and kept the middle. Three of the popups wanted more height than
the window's own minimum inner height of 480 (`main.rs`), so this was never a small-screen edge
case; it was the ordinary case on a window anybody had made short.

Two helpers fix it, and both take the **viewport** as their term of reference:

- `popup_max_height(ctx)` — the window's height less a gutter at each end, floored at 240. Every
  popup declares it.
- `list_height(ctx, chrome, want)` — how tall a popup's scrolling middle may be, where `chrome`
  is everything in the popup that is not the list. `want` is never exceeded, so a taller window
  gives a popup the list it was designed with rather than a longer one. The floor is 56, two
  rows: a popup that cannot show two rows is one you have to resize the window for, and two rows
  with a foot beats six rows and no way to act on them.

**Measuring the `Ui` instead looks like the better answer and is not**, and this cost a wrong
implementation before it cost a right one. Inside an `egui::Window`, `ui.available_height()` is
effectively unbounded — the window is sizing itself from the content, so the content is not
inside anything yet — and every list quietly kept the height it already had while the code
claiming to shrink it ran on every frame. Anchoring the arithmetic on the window's own cursor
instead makes it circular: a centre-anchored popup's position depends on the height being
computed. The viewport is the one term in the sum that does not move.

**About is the exception, and it proves the rule.** Its fixed part alone — a 150px mark, a title,
five fields and a sourcing note — is taller than the window's minimum height, so no arithmetic
over what is *left* can make it fit. Its whole body scrolls instead, and only the foot is held
out of the scroll: a popup may lose the bottom of the GPL to a small window, but it must never
lose the line naming what it is and who made it.

## 5. Fixtures and tests

No new fixtures. P9 changes colours and heights, and the only part of that a test can hold is the
colours — which is exactly the part where being wrong is invisible until somebody cannot read the
screen.

| Test | What it pins |
| --- | --- |
| `the_popup_wears_its_own_three_grounds` | That the three are ordered as drawn — head and foot both darker than the body — that each keeps its distance from the text that sits on it, and that all three are blue-dominant, so a later edit cannot quietly walk them back into aubergine |

The existing contrast tests are the other half of this milestone's cover, and the point is that
**they did not have to change.** Pinning the body at the previous popup's luminance was chosen so
that every assertion P7 wrote about grounds and inks stays true word for word; a test suite that
had to be edited to accept a new colour would have proved nothing about that colour.

Nothing here tests the heights. `popup_max_height` and `list_height` are arithmetic over
`ctx.content_rect()`, and a `Context` with a meaningful viewport needs a window — which no test in
this repository can open. That is a real gap, it is the same gap P8 §5 names, and it is why the
manual checklist exists.

## Deviations

*(recorded during implementation; empty is a valid state)*

1. **This document was written after the milestone shipped, and after the tag.** P9's two commits
   went in with a `P9:` prefix and no `build/docs/P9.md` beside them, and `v1.0` was tagged and
   released in that state — so for a while the repository referred to a milestone that did not
   exist, discoverable only by somebody grepping `build/docs` and finding nothing. Three
   documents running have now recorded "the implementation came first" (P6 Deviation 23, P7
   Deviation 1, P8 Deviation 1); this is the first to record that the *document* came after the
   release as well, which is worse and worth saying plainly.

2. **`ctx.screen_rect()` does not exist in egui 0.36.** The first implementation of
   `popup_max_height` used it, which is the name the API had elsewhere, and it failed to compile
   with E0599. `ctx.content_rect()` is the current spelling. A trivial fault, recorded only
   because the corrected line looks obvious and the wrong one looked obvious too.

3. **The first height fix ran on every frame and did nothing at all.** Measuring
   `ui.available_height()` inside an auto-sized `egui::Window` returns something effectively
   unbounded, so `min()` against it never bit and every list kept the height it already had. It
   was caught by looking at the program rather than by a failing test, because there is no test
   here that can open a window. The trap is now written into `list_height`'s own doc comment so
   the next person to reach for the obvious answer meets the reason it is wrong before they try
   it.

4. **The maker's chosen colours are not the shipped colours.** `#0F52BA`, `#4682B4` and `#0047AB`
   were given precisely, marked on a screenshot, and all three were changed before shipping. The
   change is confined to luminance and the hue is preserved exactly, the contrast measurements in
   §1 are the whole argument, and the original three are named in the CORE edit above so the
   record shows what was asked for as well as what was built. A milestone that quietly alters what
   the maker specified and does not say so is the failure this ledger exists to prevent.

5. **A fourth CORE edit was found by writing the apply-list, not by writing this document.**
   §4's popup list still calls New Archive *"Aubergine in dress"*, which P9 made false in its
   first commit. §4 of this file had been written, checked and considered complete before
   anybody went looking through CORE line by line for every sentence the recolouring touched.
   The lesson is small and repeatable: a milestone that changes a colour should grep the
   authoritative document for the colour's *name*, not only for the row that holds its value.
